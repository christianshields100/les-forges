Energy and heat · the arithmetic behind the numbers

Every megawatt it uses, none from Brittany, and the heat sold for less than gas.

"Bringing their own power" is read literally and held to 100%: the campus never imports. It is connected to the grid only to export. That removes the objection that decided Marseille and Le Bourget, and it puts a power station next to homes, which the rest of this page has to answer. Everything marked derived needs an engineer's confirmation before it is more than a design target.

Why keep a grid link at all

A campus with no grid link at all was considered and rejected.

A 100 MW load with no grid connection loses all the regional benefit, in a region that produced 31% of its own electricity in 2024, and needs more redundancy for the same reliability. Export-only keeps the benefit and keeps the promise: RTE's meter at the 90 kV La Janais post shows zero import every month, and the surplus, up to 80 MW when every unit runs, is sold into Brittany.

The cost of that promise is a gas-fired station running around the clock, with its emissions published in full.

Why a gas plant, and why not Landivisiau again

Three ways to keep the brief's promise were looked at. One holds up.

Landivisiau, the 446 MW gas plant in Finistère, was fought for a decade before it ran in 2022: it was imposed on a village by a regional pact, its heat was an afterthought, and its fuel was gas with no date for anything else. Three things differ here. The station sits on an empty lot in an activity zone, 520 m from the nearest home, in the corner of the lot furthest from homes on both sides. Part of its heat is sold, and that is the reason the network can reach the three communes. And the fuel is dated: 20% biomethane at opening, 50% by 2035, with the actual invoices audited and published each year.

Cooling

Dry and closed. Water is the top reason data centers get refused. This design uses almost none.

Liquid cooling to the racks, oversized dry coolers run at low fan speed for noise, and air-cooled condensers at the station. Total water under 10,000 m³ a year, none of it for cooling (derived from CyrusOne Wissous at 850 m³ a year for 60 MW and Telehouse Marseille at 250 m³). The comparables scale to 1,000 to 1,500 m³ for 100 MW; 10,000 m³ is the commitment ceiling, leaving room for washrooms, cleaning and construction-phase use. An evaporative design would use about 1.9 million m³ a year (derived: about 1.8 litres per kWh of facility load, 120 MW, 8,760 hours). Rainwater is harvested for the rest.

Drought orders in Ille-et-Vilaine (2022, 2023, June 2026) do not touch the campus. Loi 2026-403 lets authorities refuse permits where water is under structural stress; a dry-cooled campus gives them no reason to.

Heat

Waste heat from the engines (jacket and exhaust) and from the buildings (liquid-cooled racks give 40 to 60 °C water) goes into a new heat network from the campus to the nearest streets and to public buildings in the three communes. None of it is sold to the factory: the existing ENGIE biomass loop on the industrial site (8 MW, 3 km, 11 substations, 30 GWh a year) keeps serving it.

The tariff

Why heating and not electricity: the campus cannot sell electricity to households; that is the supplier's role at the grid's price. It can sell heat at a price it sets. So the resident's benefit lands on the heating bill.

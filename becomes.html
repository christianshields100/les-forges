Design what the project becomes

Three low buildings, a power station and a public park on three empty lots.

Three two-storey data buildings sit beside a compact heat and power station that runs the campus and exports its surplus to Brittany. All of it stands on one 5.5 ha lot. The station takes the corner furthest from homes on both sides: 520 m from the nearest house to the south and 640 m from the nearest to the north (measured). The nearest home to any building is 400 m away, at le Bois Noir. The northern lot, 200 m from the houses of rue de la Pommerais, holds no building and becomes a public park. The heat goes first to the nearest streets and to eight public buildings, schools, sports halls and a care home among them, then to every home offered a connection, and none of it to the factory; and part of the project belongs to the communes: a local benefit fund residents and their communes run and a heat co-op they own.

How the energy works

A 180 to 200 MW combined heat-and-power station, with one spare unit so maintenance never cuts supply, runs the campus around the clock; a 100 MW battery carries it through faults. The campus never imports. A 90 kV connection at the La Janais post is used only to export: the operating company commits to zero import, RTE's meter verifies it monthly and any import is published with its cause, and the surplus, 0 to 80 MW depending on how many units are running, is sold to a region that produced 31% of its own electricity in 2024.

On natural gas the station emits about 530,000 tonnes of CO₂ a year running the campus alone, and 800,000 to 890,000 tonnes when it exports at full output (derived, at the ADEME factor of 0.227 t per MWh). The number is published every year, and it falls on a fixed fuel schedule, 20% biomethane at opening and 50% of the fuel by 2035, about 40% less carbon (derived), while the heat network displaces gas boilers in thousands of homes. The station and the servers are air-cooled, so neither draws on local water.

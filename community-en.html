The neighbors' charter

For every household on the nearest streets, within about 1 km of the lots, and, for its construction clauses, every household within 300 m of the D34 approach. Signed by the operating company and the household; countersigned by the commune.

  1. At night the campus may add at most 3 dB(A) to the background measured at your façade before construction, and its own sound never exceeds 40 dB(A), whichever is stricter (derived target; acoustician confirms before the permit). The night background is measured at the façade before construction; after opening the acoustician measures the campus's contribution with everything running (residual-level method, NF S31-010) and publishes it every three months.
  2. You are first in line for the heat network, at the lower of €54 per MWh or 50% of the gas price, for 15 years (derived; confirmed before the permit application is filed; subject to the Rennes Métropole concession).
  3. No construction lorry uses the streets of Saint-Jacques-de-la-Lande or the D177; traffic comes from the south on the D34, 7:00 to 19:00 on weekdays.
  4. If your home is within 300 m of the D34 approach, lorries will pass you. For those homes the charter adds a speed limit, a wheel wash at the site exit, dust and noise monitoring on the D34 published live, and the same 48-hour contact.
  5. A named person answers you within 48 hours, by phone or at the office on the industrial site.
  6. You hold a seat on the monitoring committee. What the committee can stop is agreed with you in week 3, before signing.
  7. Your view is rendered before work starts, and the planted edge facing your street is kept for the life of the campus.
  8. Anonymised sales within 1 km are published every year. If you put your home up for sale, an independent valuation is paid for by the campus.
  9. Your household gets a founding share in the heat co-op, paid by the campus company, and a vote for the fund's resident board seats.

Draft text, on the table in the week 2 workshop. Residents' edits go into the change log. The €54 price and the 40 dB(A) ceiling are derived; both are confirmed before the permit application is filed, and a household that has signed can withdraw if either changes. Depends on: the commune (countersignature); the Rennes Métropole concession (the heat price); the homeowner whose façade carries the meter; legal drafting (the fund's share purchase).

Noise and the view

Will I hear fans all night, and will I see a grey box from my window?

40dB(A)

At night the campus may add at most 3 dB(A) to the background measured at the nearest home's façade before construction, and its own sound never exceeds 40 dB(A), whichever is stricter (derived target; acoustician confirms before the permit). 40 dB(A) is the WHO outdoor night guideline, used here as a ceiling for the campus alone; 3 dB(A) is a change most people would not notice outdoors, and stricter than the legal night emergence of 4 dB(A).

How
The station sits in the corner of its lot furthest from homes on both sides: 520 m from the nearest house to the south, 640 m from the nearest to the north. The nearest façade, at le Bois Noir, is 400 m from the nearest building, so the campus is designed to 40 dB(A) there: about 8 dB(A) quieter than a comparable campus, Olds, after its noise controls (derived). An enclosed engine hall; oversized, low-speed dry coolers; acoustic barriers; generator tests only in daytime, on a published calendar.
The view
Buildings no taller than 20 m, well under the airport's 45 m ceiling. The nearest house to a building is 400 m away. The northern lot, the one facing the nearest houses, holds no building and becomes a public park. The view from the D177 verge by rue du Manoir is drawn before anything is built.
Checked
An independent meter on a façade at le Bois Noir, the nearest home to a campus building, publishes the total level, live. The night background is measured at the façade before construction; after opening the acoustician measures the campus's contribution with everything running (residual-level method, NF S31-010) and publishes it every three months. Annual anonymised sales within 1 km are published.

Objection register, rows 1, 12 and 13. 40 dB(A) is the WHO night guideline; the 3 decibel limit comes from the 1997 ministerial order on industrial noise (3 to 4 dB(A) depending on the existing background). An acoustician confirms both before the permit.

Water

We have had drought orders three summers out of five. Will it drink our water?

10,000m³ a year, at most

None of it for cooling. The servers are cooled by a closed liquid loop and dry coolers; the station uses air-cooled condensers. A conventional evaporative design would use about 1.9 million m³ a year (derived).

How
No cooling towers, no evaporation. Rainwater from the roofs covers most of what remains (washrooms, cleaning). The campus is not a drinking-water customer in a drought.
Checked
The intake meter, published. Drought orders, like those of 2022, 2023 and June 2026, do not touch a dry-cooled campus.

Objection register, row 2. Derived from comparable French sites (850 m³ a year at 60 MW at Wissous; 250 m³ a year at Marseille), which scale to 1,000 to 1,500 m³ here; 10,000 m³ is the ceiling, leaving room for washrooms, cleaning and the construction phase.

Power

Brittany already imports most of its electricity. Will my bill go up to feed it?

0MW imported

The campus makes every megawatt it uses: a 180 to 200 MW heat-and-power station with a spare unit, and a 100 MW battery for faults. It is connected to the grid only to sell: up to 80 MW of surplus when every unit runs, into a region that produced 31% of its own electricity in 2024. The operating company commits to zero import; RTE's meter verifies it monthly, and any import is published with its cause.

The cost
On natural gas the station emits about 530,000 tonnes of CO₂ a year running the campus alone, and up to 890,000 tonnes when it exports at full output (derived). The number is published every year. Biomethane makes up 20% of the fuel at opening and 50% by 2035, bought under contract with guarantees of origin on the national market, not only from Brittany, and the share is published every year: about 40% less carbon (derived; target, to be costed). At 50% the campus alone would use more biomethane than all of Brittany produced in 2025, 1,092 GWh (derived, NaTran). No field of diesel generators: one small emergency generator to restart the station, its tank underground, tested in daytime.
Checked
The grid operator's meter at the 90 kV substation, published monthly, shows zero import. An annual emissions report is built from audited fuel invoices.

Objection register, rows 3, 4 and 9. Electricity figures: Insee Conjoncture Bretagne n° 54, Bilan économique 2024 (June 2025). Full arithmetic on the energy page.

Heat and your bill

Fine, but what do we actually get?

50% below gas on the nearest streets

Heat from the servers and the station goes into a new network to the nearest homes and public buildings, not to the factory. For a gas-heated house that is about €750 a year less on the nearest streets, about €450 for the first homes connected, and about €150 for everyone else, at least 10% below the gas bill and guaranteed in the concession (derived).

How
About 32 to 35 GWh a year at opening: about 30 GWh for a first residential extension to the nearest streets, about 2,000 homes, and 2 to 5 GWh for eight public buildings, among them École Eugène Pottier, the Collège de Fontenay, two sports halls and the Résidence de la Poterie care home (derived). All of it is new pipe, and none of it goes to the factory. Up to 300 GWh a year by years 3 to 5, about 20,000 gas-heated homes' worth (derived): every home offered a connection, the three communes first, then neighboring communes as the Rennes Métropole concession sets. The charter-zone price is the lower of €54 per MWh or half the gas price, for 15 years.
Also
Local taxes of about €8 to 10 million a year, mostly to the metropolitan authority (derived; a tax adviser confirms). The bill is published annually.
Checked
The price is written into the network contract. A bill comparison is published every year, and the meter where the heat enters the network shows what was delivered.

Objection register, row 7. Prices: AMORCE 2024 network survey (€104.7/MWh) and Effy 2025 gas bill (€1,512 for 14,000 kWh). Tiers and arithmetic on the energy page.

Jobs and skills

This area has lost thousands of jobs. A data centre employs forty people.

~320jobs supported, ~80 direct

Both numbers, because the split matters: about 80 people on the campus payroll, about 320 in total once the contractors, the heat network and the station are counted (derived: a comparable French filing's 1:4 ratio applied to the 80). Our target is half the hires from the area, reached through France Travail, the academy and insertion hours in the building contracts; we publish the share every year, and nobody is turned down for where they live. 800 to 1,500 people on site at the peak of construction.

How
A technician academy in the incubator building on the industrial site, open before the campus is, with first places for people already working on the site next door and bursaries paid by the local benefit fund. The target is half of the campus hires from the area, published every year.
Checked
An employment report every year, by postcode, with what is counted stated.

Objection register, rows 5 and 16. Ratio derived from Digital Realty's Marseille CNDP filing; construction range from Cortex Construct.

Construction and the school

Two years of lorries past the school gate.

0lorries through Saint-Jacques or on the D177; 230 homes along the D34 covered by the charter

Construction traffic comes from the south: off the N137, along the D34 and through the industrial zone, straight into the lots. Never on the D177, never through the streets to the north (the route on the D34 is subject to the Département, which owns it). The D34 is a departmental road along the northern edge of Chartres-de-Bretagne: the nearest home is 45 m from it and about 230 homes are within 300 m (derived from IGN building data). Those households are covered by the charter's construction clauses. The route stays 1.6 km from École Eugène Pottier.

How
7:00 to 19:00 on weekdays, for 24 to 36 months. An air-quality monitor at École Eugène Pottier, another at the Brocéliande nursery school, the nearest school to the D34 approach, and an air and noise monitor on the D34 itself, all published live for the whole build and after. A soil survey of the lots is published before any transfer; one closed service station sits 75 m from the project's coordinates, and cleaning it up is part of the project. Afterwards, about 300 vehicle trips a day (derived from the building floor area), all from the south.
Checked
The route and hours are written into the permit and the charter. The monitor's data is public. A named contact answers within 48 hours.

Objection register, rows 8 and 10. Traffic estimate from the US trip-rate handbook (ITE) via the Willow Lakes traffic report; soil records from Géorisques. Test the route against your own address at the top of the page.

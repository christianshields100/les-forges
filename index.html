A data center the community can hold to its word.

It brings its own power, heats your homes for less than gas, pays at least €1M a year into a fund residents and their communes run, and gives the lot facing your houses back as a park. It uses no water for cooling, keeps the neighbors' nights quiet, and trains local people for the jobs. Every commitment is a number the community can check.

Design what the project becomes

Three low buildings and a power station on one 5.5 ha lot: the nearest home is 400 m from a building and 520 m from the station.

Heat for every home offered a connection (subject to the Rennes Métropole concession), a station that exports its surplus to a region importing two-thirds of its electricity, a public park on the lot between the buildings and the houses, and a stake: a local benefit fund residents and their communes run and a heat co-op they own. The site

La Janais is an industrial site south of Rennes, between the airport and the Rennes–Nantes road, with three communes of about 31,000 people around it. Brittany produced 31% of its own electricity in 2024, and Ille-et-Vilaine has been under drought orders repeatedly since 2022, most recently in June 2026.

Recent French data center projects at Le Bourget, Marseille and Wissous were fought on water, generators, noise and who benefits. Each of those questions gets a design decision and a number here. The campus uses three empty lots, 16 hectares, on the site's western edge: one in the public ZAC La Janais, bought from its developer, and two the factory's owner would release. The factory next to them keeps running.
